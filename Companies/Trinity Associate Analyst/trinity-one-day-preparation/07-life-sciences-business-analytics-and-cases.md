## §7.1 The pharmaceutical value chain (ADDITIONAL — 60-second version)

**Discover** (research) → **Develop** (clinical trials, phases I–III) → **Regulate** (approval: FDA/EMA/CDSCO) → **Manufacture** → **Commercialize** (market access, launch, sales & marketing, lifecycle management) → **Genericize** (patent expiry, "LoE").

- Time to market: typically 10+ years; cost heavily front-loaded → single products can make or break a company.
- **Patent cliff / LoE (loss of exclusivity):** when exclusivity ends, generics enter and price/share collapse — the industry's biggest forecasting event.
- Trinity's world is the **commercial** end: before/after launches — surveys, market research, dashboards, recommendations.

## §7.2 The four stakeholders (+ two you'll meet in surveys)

| Stakeholder | Who | What they care about | Commercial term |
|---|---|---|---|
| Patient | The user | Outcomes, access, cost | Adherence, persistence |
| Physician / HCP | Prescriber | Efficacy, safety, ease, patient fit | **TRx / NRx**, prescribing deciles |
| Payer | Insurance/insurer, government | Budget, cost-effectiveness | Formulary, reimbursement |
| Provider | Hospital/clinic systems | Pathways, budgets, procurement | Institutional access |
| (PBM) | Pharmacy benefit manager | Rebates, utilization management | Formulary tiers (AWARENESS) |
| (KOL) | Key opinion leader | Evidence, guidelines | Advisory boards, publications (AWARENESS) |

**Metrics you must recognize:** **TRx** = total prescriptions · **NRx** = new prescriptions (new patients) · **NBRx** = new-to-brand (new to *this* brand) · share = brand ÷ market · **call activity** = rep visits · **share of voice** = promotional share. Cross-check: seen in Mock B as RepID/TRx/Calls — the same vocabulary.

## §7.3 The 8-step case framework (memorize this order)

1. **Clarify the objective.** "Should we enter?" vs "Why is share falling?" are different cases. Ask 1–2 sharp questions, then state your plan.
2. **Structure the problem.** Tree it: e.g. *revenue = patients × diagnosis × treatment × brand share × price*. Say the tree out loud.
3. **Identify metrics & data needed.** For each branch of the tree — the market sizing inputs ([`02`](02-aptitude-reasoning-and-data-interpretation.md)), survey answers ([`04`](04-statistics-survey-and-market-research.md)), or internal sales.
4. **Calculate.** Only to the precision the decision needs; round loudly and correctly ("≈ $265M").
5. **Insights.** The *so-what* of each number — not a data dump.
6. **Business implications.** What it means for investment, risk, organization.
7. **Recommendation.** One clear action, with the first step and a metric to track.
8. **Risks, assumptions, missing data.** State limits unprompted — senior analysts do this; juniors don't.

Interview line to remember: **"So what? — therefore, we should…"** Every number must earn its place.

## §7.4 Market size, share, and forecasting (compact toolbox)

- **Market sizing (funnel):** population → diagnosed → treated → eligible for therapy → on this class → brand share. Multiply down; each stage is a percentage of the previous.
- **Revenue forecast:** eligible patients × penetration × annual per-patient revenue (net of discounts).
- **Share:** brand ÷ market, in both units (TRx) and value — they differ when prices differ.
- **Growth:** market growth vs brand growth determines share direction ([`02` §2.9](02-aptitude-reasoning-and-data-interpretation.md)).
- **Three scenarios:** low / base / high penetration — present all three in a case; interviewers reward the range.

## §7.5 Patient journey & adoption (OLDER JD context)

**Patient journey (commercial lens):** symptoms → physician visit → diagnosis → treatment decision → fill prescription → adherence → persistence/switch. Commercial intervention points: awareness (DTC/education), diagnosis support, access (formulary), adherence programs.

**Adoption curve:** innovators → early adopters → majority → laggards. New products ramp on an S-curve; surveys capture *intent* before behavior catches up (**stated intent overstates early adoption** — a classic case trap).

**Adoption drivers/barriers to probe in any case:** efficacy vs standard of care, safety/tolerability, dosing convenience, guideline position, formulary/reimbursement access, price, awareness, specialist vs primary-care prescribing split.

**Segmentation (OLDER JD context):** cut the market by need, behavior (prescribing deciles — top decile drives a huge share), specialty, geography, channel. Segments must be *actionable* — that's the mantra.

## §7.6 Worked Case 1 — Market entry (do this one with me)

**Prompt.** PharmaCo has completed Phase III for **Glycostat**, a new oral adjunct for Type-2 diabetes in Country X. Entry requires a $80M launch investment. Should they launch? (PRACTICE case — fictional numbers, internally consistent.)

**1–2) Objective & structure.** Seasoned launch decision → *peak-year revenue vs investment*. Tree: **patients → treated → eligible → penetration → annual cost per patient**.

**3) Data given.** Diagnosed adults with T2D: 6.0M · on pharmacotherapy: 70% · eligible for the new class: 35% of treated · expected peak penetration: 15% · annual therapy cost: $1,200 (net).

**4) Calculate.**
- Treated: 6.0M × 0.70 = **4.2M**
- Eligible: 4.2M × 0.35 = **1.47M**
- Peak patients: 1.47M × 0.15 = **220,500**
- Peak revenue: 220,500 × $1,200 = **≈ $264.6M**

**5) Insights.** Peak revenue is ~3.3× the launch investment; at a 70% gross margin, ~$185M gross profit at peak → the $80M is recovered in ~**0.43 of one peak year's gross profit** (call it payback within 2–3 years given a real ramp). Sensitivity: at 10% (not 15%) penetration, revenue = **$176.4M** (−33%) — the case hinges on the penetration assumption.

**6) Implications.** Attractive on base-case; the biggest swing factors are reimbursement (access drives penetration more than efficacy in mature classes) and the incumbent's response.

**7) Recommendation.** Proceed to launch, stage the $80M (build commercial infrastructure after first access wins), pilot in 3 regions, track penetration vs the 10/15/20% scenario band.

**8) Risks/assumptions.** Penetration is assumed, not earned; net price excludes discounts/rebates; no competitor price war modeled; eligibility based on label that regulators may narrow.

## §7.7 Case 2 — Product-adoption decline (solve this one, ≤ 25 min)

**Prompt.** **Bronchovent** (inhaled, launched 3 years ago) fell from **48,000 TRx in Q4 2025 to 42,240 in Q1 2026**. The commercial head wants a diagnosis and plan. You have the following tables (PRACTICE):

| Dimension | Q4 2025 | Q1 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| TRx total | 48,000 | 42,240 |
| Top-3 of 24 territories | 24,300 | 20,840 |
| Remaining 21 territories | 23,700 | 21,400 |
| Calls per rep (affected territories) | 480 | 340 |
| Vacant rep positions | 0 | 2 of 24 |

*(Solve: size the decline; decompose by territory; form hypotheses; recommend. Then compare with §7.8.)*

## §7.8 Case 2 — model solution

**Size:** decline = 48,000 − 42,240 = **5,760 TRx = −12.0%**.

**Decompose.** Top-3 territories fell 24,300 → 20,840 = **−3,460** → **60.1% of the total decline** (3,460/5,760) from 3 of 24 territories. The other 21 declined **2,300** combined (−9.7%).

**Hypotheses (structure before blame).** ① Effort: calls per rep in affected territories −29.2% (480→340) + 2 vacancies → coverage gap. ② Competitive: new entrant/competitor launch in those regions (check market: if total market fell only ~3%, the brand lost share where the competitor landed). ③ Access/policy: formulary change or prior-auth in those regions. ④ Data artifact: stock-out/backorder or reporting lag.

**Insights.** The decline is **concentrated, not broad**: fix 3 territories, not the whole field force. Effort metrics (calls, vacancies) line up with the timing in exactly those territories; market-wide seasonality can explain at most the residual 2,300.

**Recommendation.** (a) Fill the 2 vacancies within X weeks; (b) reallocate calls from stable territories to the affected 3; (c) if a competitor launched there, counter with targeted physician education on the head-to-head profile; (d) weekly tracking of TRx + calls + NBRx to watch the recovery. **Metric to track:** affected-territory TRx back to ≥ 7,800/month within 2 quarters.

**Risks/assumptions.** Assumes no national access change (validate!); assumes call direction is causal — test by tracking before/after reallocation; one quarter is thin evidence — confirm with a second quarter before major reorg.

## §7.9 Physician survey interpretation (bridge to file [`04`](04-statistics-survey-and-market-research.md))

When a case gives you survey results: (1) check n and fielding (who responded?); (2) read distributions, not averages; (3) compare segments (specialty/decile/region); (4) separate **stated intent** ("would prescribe") from **revealed behavior** (TRx); (5) convert to action. Example line: *"43% of surveyed physicians would prescribe at launch — historically intent overstates adoption by roughly a third before access settles; plan for ~30% trial."* (**ADDITIONAL**.)

## §7.10 Glossary (unfamiliar terms — skim twice)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| TRx / NRx / NBRx | Total / new / new-to-brand prescriptions |
| HCP | Health care professional (physician, NP, PA) |
| Payer / PBM | Who pays / who manages the drug benefit (formulary tiers, rebates) |

| Market access | Getting + keeping reimbursement/formulary position |

| Custom vs syndicated | Client-specific survey vs multi-client panel/audit |
| FIE | Fielded in-depth interview (qualitative) |
