# Mock Interview Engine

Mocks are run **live, in conversation** — this file is the menu and the rules, not pre-written transcripts, because a real mock has to react to your actual answers to be worth anything.

---

## How a Mock Works

1. You pick (or I suggest) a mock type from the list below.
2. I ask one question at a time and wait for your answer — no peeking at the "correct" answer first.
3. I evaluate your answer, note gaps, and ask a follow-up that escalates in difficulty — the same drilling-down technique documented in `INTERVIEWER_FOLLOWUPS.md`.
4. This continues for the session; at the end, I give you a scorecard: what was strong, what had gaps, and specifically what to review before the next attempt.
5. I do not reveal the "ideal" answer before you've attempted a question — that defeats the purpose.

## Recommended Order

Given your target roles and project strengths: start with **Mock 10 (Resume Deep Dive)** first — it's both your highest-value prep and the fastest way to surface which `NEEDS VERIFICATION` items you still need to check against your actual code before going further. Follow with **Mock 9 (Distributed Systems)** and **Mock 11 (Systems/Linux/C)** to stress-test the two hardest technical areas, then round out with the language/framework-specific mocks based on what roles you're actually applying to, and finish your prep cycle with **Mock 12 (Final Mixed)** as a dress rehearsal.
